# Product Management Workflow: The 6D Process

Source: [Product_Management_Workflow.pdf](<../../Scalar java backend contents/Product_Management_Workflow.pdf>) (4 pages)

The source describes product management as a collaborative six-stage process: Discover, Define, Design, Develop, Deliver, and Debrief. Each stage involves different evidence, collaborators, and tools; the stages feed into one another rather than acting as isolated handoffs.

```text
Discover -> Define -> Design -> Develop -> Deliver -> Debrief
    ^                                                  |
    +---------------- learn and iterate ---------------+
```

## 1. Discover

**Purpose:** understand users, the market, behavior, and the size of an opportunity.

Activities in the source:

- Talk to users.
- Analyze product data.
- Research the market.
- Estimate opportunity size.

**Collaborators:** user research, business, design, engineering, and customer experience.

**Tools named:** UXCam for session recordings, Mixpanel for product analytics, Lookback for contextual inquiry, and Typeform for surveys.

**Output:** evidence-backed opportunities and a clearer view of who experiences the problem.

## 2. Define

**Purpose:** turn research into a prioritized, scoped problem and a longer-term direction.

Activities:

- Continue user conversations and research.
- Analyze findings.
- Prioritize opportunities.
- Scope the work.

**Collaborators:** user research, business, design, engineering, legal, finance, customer experience, and operations.

**Tools named:** Mixpanel, Draw.io for wireframing, Miro for user-story mapping, and Trello for project management.

**Output:** a shared problem statement, priority, scope, and constraints.

## 3. Design

**Purpose:** develop and evaluate a solution with a clear rationale.

Activities include supporting design choices with analysis and critical reasoning, then testing usability.

**Collaborators:** user research, business, design, engineering, and QA.

**Tools named:** Lookback for usability testing, Figma for design, and Metabase for analysis.

**Output:** a tested design and explanation of how it addresses the defined problem.

## 4. Develop

**Purpose:** translate the selected direction into implementable work while preserving the long-term product vision.

Activities:

- Write user stories and product requirements documents (PRDs).
- Support engineering with context about the long-term direction and scalable design.

**Collaborators:** design, engineering, and QA.

**Tools named:** Coda for documentation and Jira for agile sprint tracking.

**Output:** a planned and implemented increment aligned to the intended user outcome.

## 5. Deliver

**Purpose:** test, release, and measure the solution.

Activities:

- Test the release.
- Prioritize bugs and make release trade-offs.
- Design and set up experiments.
- Analyze experiment results.

**Collaborators:** design, engineering, and QA.

**Tools named:** Jira for bug tracking, Mixpanel and Metabase for analytics, and Lookback and Typeform for user feedback.

**Output:** a safe release plus evidence about how users respond.

## 6. Debrief

**Purpose:** assess impact, learn from outcomes, and decide what to do next.

Activities:

- Document impact analysis and root-cause analysis (RCA).
- Decide whether to scale up, scale down, or change the work.
- Ensure tracking is in place for any scale-up.

**Collaborators:** business, design, engineering, and QA.

**Tools named:** Coda for documentation and Mixpanel/Metabase for tracking.

**Output:** a recorded decision and learning that informs the next discovery cycle.

## Connecting the stages

- Discover gathers signals; Define chooses which problem to address.
- Design tests possible solutions; Develop makes the selected solution buildable.
- Deliver creates real-world evidence; Debrief interprets it.
- If evidence contradicts an assumption, return to discovery or redefine the problem.

The tools are examples from the source, not a required product-management stack. Select tools that support the work and keep research, decisions, and outcomes accessible to collaborators.

## Quick self-check

1. What distinguishes Discover from Define?
2. Which stage turns a problem into a tested design?
3. What kinds of trade-offs happen during Deliver?
4. What belongs in a Debrief?
5. How can analytics, user research, design, engineering, and QA contribute at different stages?
6. Why should the workflow loop back after results are measured?
